Match Preview

Head-to-head History

Mexico and South Korea have a rich history, having met 14 times. Mexico holds a significant advantage with 8 wins, compared to South Korea's 4, with 2 draws. Their most recent World Cup meeting was in 2018, where Mexico won 2-1 in a highly competitive group stage match. Mexico also won a memorable 1998 World Cup fixture 3-1. Historically, Mexico has been the dominant force in this matchup, particularly in competitive settings. However, South Korea has won several friendlies in recent decades, proving they are always a dangerous opponent for El Tri.

Form & Ranking

Mexico is ranked 14th in the world and has been in dominant form at home. South Korea is ranked 22nd and remains a powerhouse in Asia. Both teams enter this match following strong opening performances. Mexico’s squad is healthy and high on confidence, while South Korea is led by a firing Son Heung-min. No major injuries are reported for either side. The match in Guadalajara will be played in intense heat and altitude, conditions that Mexico is very familiar with, giving them a physical edge over the visiting South Koreans.

What to Expect

This is anticipated to be one of the most exciting matches of the group stage. Mexico will look to dominate possession and use the crowd's energy to fuel their high press. South Korea will look to exploit any defensive lapses from Mexico with their elite speed on the counter-attack. Pundits at ESPN and various Korean outlets predict a narrow 2-1 win for Mexico, though a 1-1 draw is also seen as a likely outcome. The Athletic highlights that the duel between Mexico's defenders and Son Heung-min will be the key to the entire match.
