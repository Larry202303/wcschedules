Match Preview

Head-to-head History

England and Ghana have met only once in their history, a 2011 friendly match at Wembley that ended in a 1-1 draw. That match is fondly remembered by Ghanaian fans for Asamoah Gyan's late equalizer. This fixture in Boston is their first competitive meeting and only their second ever encounter. England has a long history of World Cup success, while Ghana is one of Africa’s most consistent and successful teams in the tournament’s recent history. The 1-1 record in their only meeting suggests that Ghana can hold their own against the Three Lions on a neutral ground.

Form & Ranking

England is ranked 4th in the world and arrives with one of their strongest-ever squads, led by Harry Kane and Jude Bellingham. Ghana is ranked 48th, boasting a young and talented team with players like Mohammed Kudus. England’s form has been dominant in Europe, while Ghana has been in good form in African qualifying. Both teams enter the match with mostly healthy squads, though England is managing the workload of some key players. England enters as the clear favorite, but Ghana’s history of performing against top nations in the World Cup makes them a dangerous opponent.

What to Expect

Expect England to control possession and look to break down Ghana’s defense through patient buildup and the individual quality of their attackers. Ghana will look to use their speed and athleticism to catch England on the break. Pundits at the BBC and various African outlets predict a 2-1 win for England, citing their superior depth and tactical flexibility. The Athletic highlights that Ghana’s physicality could cause problems for England’s midfield. A competitive and entertaining match is anticipated, with both teams likely to find the back of the net in Boston.
