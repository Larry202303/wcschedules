The 2026 World Cup, in your time zone

The 2026 FIFA World Cup is the first to feature 48 teams and 104 matches, co-hosted by the United States, Canada and Mexico from June 11 to July 19. With games kicking off across North America's many time zones — and broadcast around the world — figuring out exactly when a match starts where you live can be surprisingly confusing.

That's the problem this site solves. Every kickoff is automatically converted to your local time, so you never have to subtract hours from "ET" or "Mexico City time" in your head. Pick your timezone once and the whole schedule — group stage, Round of 32, quarter-finals and the Final at MetLife Stadium — updates instantly. You can also browse the schedule by host city, by national team, or by group, and check what channel the tournament is on in your country.

How kickoff times work at a multi-country World Cup

Because matches are played from the US East Coast to the Pacific and down into Mexico, a single matchday can span four local time zones. A 12:00 noon kickoff in Los Angeles is 3:00 PM in New York and the early hours of the next morning in Asia. Rather than memorize offsets, use the timezone switcher at the top of the page — or open one of our ready-made local-time schedules — and let the conversion happen for you. All times are based on the official FIFA fixtures and update live as scores come in.
